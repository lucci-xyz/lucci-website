Open with a paragraph or two before the first heading — this is what readers see
at the fold under the big title. No "Abstract" label needed; just start.

You can attach a numbered margin note anywhere with a sidenote. Give each one a
unique id per article.<Sidenote id="sn-1">Sidenotes float in the right margin on desktop and become tap-to-reveal notes on mobile. Links work inside them: [example](https://example.com).</Sidenote>

## Section headings become the table of contents

Every `##` heading automatically appears in the sticky left TOC with scrollspy.
Use plain sentence case. Sub-points stay in prose — avoid `###` unless necessary.

Standard markdown works everywhere: **bold**, *italic*, [links](/docs), and lists:

- Square-bulleted lists
- Keep items short

> Blockquotes render with a light left rule — use them for the one line you want
> remembered.

## Images and figures

Put image files in `public/writing/<your-slug>/` and reference them absolutely.
Wrap anything in `<Figure>` to add a small centered caption:

<Figure caption="A short caption, centered and gray, under whatever the figure holds.">
  ![Alt text for the image](/writing/your-slug/example.png)
</Figure>

For layered/lifecycle diagrams there is a ready-made component:

<Figure caption="Rows with hairlines — good for layers, lifecycles, taxonomies.">
  <ManifestDiagram
    label="Accessible description of the diagram"
    rows={[
      { name: "First", tag: "Label", desc: "One line describing this row." },
      { name: "Second", tag: "Label", desc: "One line describing this row." },
    ]}
  />
</Figure>
